# Tutorial: A Course Curriculum

Planning a course means juggling a schedule, readings, assignments, and — over
time — a record of what worked. DocuShark keeps all of it in one document: the
plan, the reading PDFs, a map of how the units connect, and a snapshot you can
archive each term. This walkthrough builds an **Intro to Astronomy** course plan.

**What you'll build:** a course plan document — a weekly schedule, readings
embedded as files, a citable reading list, a course concept-map, and a canvas
showcase of exemplary student work.

**You'll learn:** tables, **embedded files** as readings, citations, a linked
concept diagram, **archiving a document as `.docushark`**, and organizing with
collections.

**Before you start:** [open DocuShark](https://app.docushark.app).

![The course plan in Split view — the weekly schedule on the left, the concept-dependency map on the right.](/screenshots/example-course-plan.png)

## 1. Start the plan

Click **New** and name it `Intro to Astronomy — Course Plan`. Add **document
fields** for `Course`, `Term`, and `Instructor` so the header stays consistent
and you can clone the plan next term by just changing the values. See
[Document Fields](/guide/document-fields).

## 2. Lay out the schedule

Write your learning outcomes as a list, then build the **weekly schedule** as a
table — one row per week, columns for Topic, Reading, and Assignment:

| Week | Topic | Reading | Assignment |
|------|-------|---------|------------|
| 1–2 | The night sky & scale | Ch. 1 | Star-chart worksheet |
| 3–4 | The solar system | Ch. 2–3 | Planet fact-file |

Tables are first-class in the editor — see [Rich Text & Notes](/guide/rich-text-editor).

## 3. Attach the readings as files

Keep the actual readings *in* the plan so students open everything from one
place. Drag each **PDF onto the canvas** — it becomes a file card with a preview
thumbnail. Lab handouts, slide decks, and datasets work the same way. See
[Embedded Files](/guide/embedded-files).

<!-- SCREENSHOT: a canvas page with several reading PDFs embedded as preview cards -->

## 4. Cite the set texts

For the course's set texts and papers, use the **reference library** rather than
a hand-typed list: add each book or article (a **DOI** fills the details), then
drop **inline citations** into your Readings section. The bibliography generates
itself. See [Citations & References](/guide/citations).

## 5. Map how the units connect

Add a **second canvas page** named `Course Map` and diagram the arc —
*Foundations → Solar System → Stars → Galaxies → Cosmology* — with connectors
(`C`) showing prerequisites. Reference it from your prose (*"See the Course Map
for how the units connect"*) so students get the big picture. See
[Multi-Page Documents](/guide/multi-page-documents).

## 6. Showcase exemplary student work

Pin standout student projects — a star-chart annotation, a galaxy-classification
poster — to a **Showcase** area of the canvas, and highlight them for next term's
class. Everything lives beside the plan it belongs to.

## 7. Archive each term

At the end of the semester, **export the plan as a `.docushark` archive** — a
single file that bundles the prose, the diagrams, and every embedded reading. Keep
one per cohort so the course history stays intact and importable later, on web or
desktop. See [Export & Import](/guide/export-import).

Group your per-term plans into a **collection** so your whole teaching history is
one tidy shelf. See [Collections & Tags](/guide/collections).

## 8. Available on every device

Your plan syncs to your workspace — pull it up in the lecture hall, at home, or on
your phone, and mark it [available offline](/guide/offline-and-sync) for a
no-signal classroom.

## What you learned

You built a course plan that holds its own schedule, readings, concept-map, and
student showcase — citable, archivable per term, and synced everywhere.
